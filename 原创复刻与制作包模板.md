# Production Package

Read this reference only after the original-video teardown is complete or when the user explicitly requests an edit plan or production package.

## 1. Edit optimization table

| Source time | Operation | Reason | New placement | Execution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Allowed operations:

- `KEEP`: retain
- `TRIM`: shorten
- `REMOVE`: delete
- `MOVE`: change order
- `BROLL`: insert real evidence footage
- `SUBTITLE`: revise subtitle
- `AUDIO`: adjust speech, music, or sound
- `REVIEW`: needs manual frame-by-frame confirmation

Preserve meaning, complete words, sentence endings, natural pauses, visual continuity, product-model consistency, and evidence authenticity. Explain every edit. Use approximate ranges plus `REVIEW` when the exact cut is uncertain.

Then produce an original-versus-optimized comparison covering:

- recommended duration
- recommended opening
- retained content
- repeated or weak content to remove
- shots that need to be filmed
- subtitle and music changes
- revised CTA
- risks to truthfulness or buyer trust

If actual editing and export are available and authorized, perform them and verify the result. Otherwise provide a CapCut/剪映-executable plan or a structured FFmpeg timeline. Never claim an MP4 exists unless it does.

## 2. Business-fit questions

After teardown, ask only missing questions and no more than three per round:

1. What is sold, and what are its real, provable advantages?
2. Who buys it, in which country, and in what role: importer, distributor, wholesaler, contractor, installer, or another buyer?
3. What real footage exists, and what should the video achieve: reach, targeted follows, direct messages, WhatsApp inquiries, catalog requests, or quote opportunities?

If the buyer type is unclear, propose plausible buyer roles based on the product and let the user choose.

## 3. Two original adaptations

Always distinguish the two versions.

### A. Structure-transfer version

Transfer the source's opening type, information order, narrative pacing, shot mechanism, and visual logic. Rebuild with the user's product, real footage, and original copy.

### B. B2B acquisition-enhanced version

Further optimize:

- correct buyer address
- procurement pain
- use value
- real factory, test, shipment, project, or process evidence
- commercial cooperation advantage
- inquiry handoff

Explain where each version is appropriate.

## 4. Shootable storyboard

| Time | Target-language spoken line | Corresponding visual | Camera position | Camera movement | Subtitle | Edit instruction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Provide:

- Chinese meaning/context
- target-market language version
- opening title
- required real footage list
- missing shots to capture
- voice, ambience, and music direction
- publishing copy
- WhatsApp or other inquiry CTA

The target language must sound natural in the local B2B context. It should not be a literal translation. Select the market language from the buyer and market; if the buyer's working language is unclear, ask rather than assuming.

## 5. Deliverables

### A. Original video analysis report

Explains what the source does, how it works, and where it is weak.

### B. Original replication shooting plan

Can be executed by factory staff or a video operator.

### C. Edit execution pack

Includes:

- recommended duration
- shot timeline
- retained and removed segments
- spoken script and subtitles
- B-roll insertion points
- pauses and audio treatment
- transitions
- CapCut/剪映 instructions
- structured FFmpeg edit plan when useful

If a finished video can actually be produced and exported, include it and a QC report separately. Otherwise deliver only executable plans.

## 6. Final quality gate

Before responding, verify:

1. The uploaded video was actually observed rather than replaced with generic knowledge.
2. Important shot claims have evidence.
3. The analysis explains mechanisms, not only visible surface behavior.
4. Original proposals fit the target buyer.
5. The shooting and editing plan is executable by an ordinary operator.
6. No price, certification, specification, capacity, case, testimonial, or evidence footage was invented.
7. Synthetic or material-altered footage is not presented as real business evidence.
8. No finished video is claimed unless it was generated and checked.

Correct unsupported claims instead of adding report length.
